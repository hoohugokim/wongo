# npj Biofilms and Microbiomes editorial framing

Source: [Aims & Scope](https://www.nature.com/npjbiofilms/aims), fetched and
read 2026-07-04. This is a general-purpose scope description for any
prospective author — it is not tailored to a specific manuscript.

## What the journal says it wants

The journal examines "the biology, ecology, and communal function of
biofilms, microbial populations, and communities, as well as derived
applications across the medical, environmental, and engineering sciences."
Topic areas covered include:

- Microbiology fundamentals: genetics, environmental effects on microbial
  behavior, evolution, and community dynamics.
- Biofilm formation mechanisms and physical/chemical properties.
- Biofilm prevention and detection strategies.
- Biofilm removal approaches — mechanical, chemical, and therapeutic
  methods.
- Biofilm control and engineering applications (industrial, medical,
  environmental).
- Host-microbiome relationships in animals, plants, and human health and
  disease.
- Environmental microbiomes, including communities in extreme environments.
- Microbiome engineering and deliberate manipulation of microbial
  communities.
- Bioinformatics tools and methods for biofilm/microbiome research.

Positioning: as an open-access member of the npj Series, the journal offers
authors "a fully open-access and more inclusive platform for publishing
their work," led by biofilms and microbiomes specialists, and aims to
"foster global partnerships with the research community and other Springer
Nature journals." Published in partnership with Nanyang Technological
University Singapore and the Singapore Centre for Environmental Life
Sciences Engineering (SCELSE).

## How to read this scope for fit

The journal spans a wide bench-to-bedside-to-environment range: pure
microbiology (genetics, evolution, community ecology) sits alongside applied
biofilm engineering (industrial fouling, medical-device biofilms, control/
removal technologies) and translational host-microbiome work (gut, plant,
disease-associated microbiomes). A manuscript fits well if it centers on
biofilm biology/behavior OR microbiome composition-function-application,
even if the underlying discipline is engineering, computational biology,
ecology, or clinical/veterinary science rather than classical microbiology.
Purely computational or bioinformatics-tool papers are explicitly in scope
("bioinformatics tools and methods") as long as the tool/method targets
biofilm or microbiome data.

Article types map onto research maturity and scope breadth: substantial
original primary research (including systematic reviews/meta-analyses) goes
in Article; narrative summaries not dominated by a single lab's work go in
Review; brief, contained findings go in Brief Communication; broad
non-technical scientific-society discussion goes in Comment or Perspective;
commissioned discussion of a scientific meeting/workshop's outputs goes in
the journal-specific Meeting Report type; formal responses to a previously
published npj Biofilms and Microbiomes paper go in Matters Arising (which
requires contacting the original authors first).

## Desk-screen considerations (inferred from journal structure, not stated
explicitly as desk-rejection criteria — treat as judgment, not a rule)

- No stated novelty/significance rubric specific to this journal was found
  in the fetched Aims & Scope or Content types pages — Nature Portfolio's
  general editorial criteria (novel, reproducible, adequately powered,
  ethically sound) apply by default via the journal's own Editorial
  policies landing page, which states it follows shared Nature Portfolio
  policies.
- The Review type's own scope rule — "not dominated by the work of a single
  laboratory, particularly not the author's own work" — functions as an
  implicit desk-screen criterion for Review submissions specifically;
  narrow single-lab summaries are a poor fit for that type (though may fit
  Perspective, Comment, or Article instead).
- The AI editorial policy distinguishes AI-as-authorship-tool (restricted,
  must be disclosed) from AI-as-subject-of-study (a manuscript that studies
  or benchmarks AI/LLM systems is not itself constrained by the authorship
  policy beyond normal disclosure of any LLM used to help write the paper)
  — relevant framing for any manuscript at the intersection of computational
  methods and biofilm/microbiome science.
