# Environmental Microbiome editorial framing

Source: [Aims and scope](https://link.springer.com/journal/40793/aims-and-scope),
[journal homepage](https://link.springer.com/journal/40793), and
[Editorial board](https://link.springer.com/journal/40793/editorial-board),
fetched and read in full 2026-07-04. This file is general-purpose scope
guidance for any manuscript considering this journal — not tied to any one
project's content.

## What the journal says it wants

Verbatim (Aims and scope): "Microorganisms can be found across all
environments on Earth; adapting to external changes, being central to
Earth's systems and cycles, an essential part of agricultural practices,
and — through applied microbiology — providing solutions to our everyday
needs. Environmental Microbiome acknowledges this universal presence and
importance and is seeking submissions addressing the varied facets of
environmental and applied microbiome research."

Explicitly listed topics (not exhaustive — "these studies include (but are
not limited to) aspects of"):
- Air, soil, and aquatic microbial ecology.
- Microbiome analyses.
- Bioremediation.
- Microbiome of the built environment.
- Geomicrobiology.
- Microbial interaction with plants and agricultural crops.
- Extreme environment microbiology and astrobiology.

**Genome sequences**: in-scope only "if they are a fully integrated aspect
of a research article which elucidates the function and role of the
microorganisms in their environmental communities" — a standalone genome-
announcement paper, without ecological/functional interpretation, does not
fit.

**Positioning** (homepage "Overview"): "The journal's ultimate goal is to
provide the microbiology community with an open access mid-level impact
journal for quality research encompassing all aspects of environmental
microbiology — from air, to soil, to water, and beyond." The journal
explicitly frames itself as favoring an "applied context that will shape
future innovation and research by connecting genomic and microbiome
analyses to their roles in the environment and human lives" — pure
descriptive/taxonomic surveys without an applied or functional angle are a
weaker fit than work that ties community composition to function, process,
or consequence (bioremediation outcome, agricultural performance,
biogeochemical cycling, human/environmental health interface, etc.).

**Speed/scale signals** (homepage metrics, not a formatting source but
useful for cover-letter framing): Journal Impact Factor 6.2 (2025), 5-year
JIF 7.3 (2025), median time to first decision 25 days, ~1.1M downloads
(2025) — a journal that markets itself on "a positive author experience
with rapid turnaround time for submissions, thorough and timely peer
review."

## Methodological rigor the editors explicitly screen for

Distinct from generic BMC boilerplate, this journal's own Research/
Methodology/Brief report Criteria sections set an unusually specific
methodological bar, useful to address proactively in a cover letter or
Methods section rather than leave for reviewers to raise:
- **Negative AND positive experimental controls** (sampling, extraction,
  PCR — plus mock-community positive controls), sequenced and deposited
  publicly. Framed explicitly around low-biomass sample risk: "Materials
  and reagent, experimental sampling and processing can introduce
  contamination (DNA or cells) that if not controlled would jeopardize the
  integrity and quality of a study."
- **MixS-compliant metadata** for any sequence data (Genome Standards
  Consortium standard), with sample identifiers matching between the
  manuscript and the repository record.
- **Compositionally-aware statistics** for any co-association network
  analysis — the journal names Pearson/Spearman correlation alone as
  insufficient, citing a specific methods literature (Gloor 2017, Friedman
  & Alm 2012, Weiss 2016, Peschel 2020, Bindels 2025).
- Consistent taxonomic nomenclature (ASM/Journal of Bacteriology
  italicization) and vocabulary ("16S rRNA gene", following Marchesi et
  al.'s recommended microbiome terminology).
- The journal separately flags the cross-journal **STREAMS guidelines**
  (standards for technical reporting in environmental and host-associated
  microbiome studies) as encouraged reading, shared with its sibling
  journal Microbiome.

A manuscript that visibly addresses these points up front — rather than
waiting for reviewer #2 to ask — reads as journal-savvy to an editor who
already knows these are the journal's known failure modes for
microbiome-adjacent submissions.

## Desk-screen considerations (inferred from journal structure and stated
policy, not an explicit desk-rejection rubric — treat as judgment)

- **Review is commissioned-only**: "Environmental Microbiome no longer
  considers unsolicited reviews" (verbatim). An unsolicited review-style
  manuscript should either be recast as a different article type (e.g., a
  Perspective, which explicitly permits "alternative interpretations of
  existing work") or should first go through a presubmission enquiry to the
  editors before drafting.
- **Environment-first framing distinguishes this journal from its BMC
  sibling, Microbiome**: this journal foregrounds air/soil/aquatic/built-
  environment/geomicrobiology/extreme-environment framing; a manuscript
  whose primary lens is host-associated or clinical microbiome work (even
  if it touches environmental exposure) is more likely a better fit for
  `quarto-manuscript-microbiome`. When a study genuinely straddles both,
  lead the cover letter with whichever framing this journal's own scope
  language echoes more closely (the environment/substrate the microbiome
  inhabits, not the host).
- **Applied/functional framing over pure survey/taxonomic description**:
  the journal's own "Overview" text pushes toward work connecting
  microbiome analysis to environmental or applied consequence
  (bioremediation, agriculture, biogeochemistry, built-environment health,
  astrobiology relevance) rather than composition-only community surveys
  with no functional or applied interpretation.
- **Genome papers need an ecological/functional hook**: a standalone genome
  sequence or genome-announcement paper without environmental-community
  interpretation does not fit the stated scope; frame any genome-centric
  submission around the organism's functional role in its environment.
- No stated novelty/significance rubric beyond the generic BMC peer-review
  criteria (scientific robustness, originality, clarity) was found in any
  fetched page — do not fabricate a more specific bar than that.
