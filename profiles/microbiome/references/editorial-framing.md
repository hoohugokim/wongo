# Microbiome (BMC) editorial framing

Source: [Aims and scope](https://link.springer.com/journal/40168/aims-and-scope),
fetched and read in full 2026-07-04. This framing is generic — written for
any prospective author evaluating fit, not tailored to any one manuscript.

## What the journal says it wants

Microbiome "encompasses studies of microbiomes colonizing humans, animals,
plants or the environment, both built and natural or manipulated, as in
agriculture." It publishes work on:
- Development and application of meta-omics approaches (metagenomics,
  metatranscriptomics, metaproteomics, metabolomics, etc.) and novel
  bioinformatics tools.
- Community/host interactions, with an explicit emphasis on
  structure-function relationships.
- Research that would "lead to substantial advances in the field."

The journal states it is "especially interested in studies that go beyond
descriptive omics surveys and include experimental or theoretical
approaches that mechanistically support proposed microbiome functions, and
establish, if possible, cause and effect relationships." This is the
journal's central, explicitly stated editorial bar: mechanism over
description.

Positioning: the journal aims to "integrate researchers with common
scientific objectives across a broad cross-section of sub-disciplines
within microbial ecology," bringing together "communities working in the
environmental, animal and biomedical microbiome arenas."

## What is explicitly out of scope

Verbatim: "Studies of individual microbial isolates/species in vivo or in
laboratory cultures without exploring the mechanisms by which they affect
the complex microbiome structures and functions will not be considered."
A manuscript studying a single organism's physiology in isolation, without
connecting it back to community-level structure or function, does not fit
this journal regardless of technical quality.

The journal also "does not consider unsolicited Reviews" — Review-type
manuscripts must be invited by the editors; do not submit one cold.

## Sibling journals and where they diverge

BMC/Springer Nature publishes several microbiome-adjacent titles; Microbiome
sits among a family that includes:
- **Environmental Microbiome** — narrower environmental-systems scope,
  its own BMC submission-guidelines profile (a separate
  `quarto-manuscript-<slug>` skill, not this one).
- **Animal Microbiome** — animal-host-focused sibling, same BMC platform
  and structural conventions (IMRaD, Declarations, single-anonymous review)
  but its own guideline pages and requirements.
- **BMC Microbiology** — broader general microbiology scope, explicitly
  named as a manuscript-transfer partner on Microbiome's own Aims and scope
  page.
- **npj Biofilms and Microbiomes** — a DIFFERENT publisher family (Nature
  Portfolio/npj series, not BMC); different structure, word-limit
  conventions, and CSL. See `quarto-manuscript-npjbiofilms` for that
  profile; do not conflate the two journals' requirements.

Authors deciding between Microbiome and one of its BMC siblings should read
each journal's own Aims and scope page rather than assume shared scope
boundaries — "microbiome" as a subject area is split across these titles
by host/environment focus and by the mechanistic-vs-descriptive bar that
Microbiome itself states explicitly.

## Desk-screen considerations (inferred from the journal's own scope
language, not a formal desk-rejection rubric — treat as judgment)

- The "mechanism, not just description" bar is Microbiome's own stated
  differentiator from purely descriptive omics-survey journals. A cover
  letter or framing paragraph that explicitly names the mechanistic
  contribution (cause-and-effect evidence, not just compositional
  correlation) speaks directly to this stated bar.
- The explicit exclusion of single-isolate/single-species lab studies means
  any manuscript centered on one organism should foreground how its
  findings connect back to community-level structure or function, or it
  risks a scope-based desk rejection regardless of technical rigor.
- No numeric novelty/significance rubric, selectivity statistics, or
  desk-rejection-rate data were found on any fetched page — Impact Factor
  (14.9, 2025) and median time-to-first-decision (24 days) are the only
  quantitative signals available from the fetched Journal homepage/info
  page; treat them as informational context, not formatting or scope rules.
